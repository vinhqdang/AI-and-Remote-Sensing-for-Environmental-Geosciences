# Recent references (2024–2026): supported claims and placement

All 30 entries below were appended to `references.bib` and checked against the Crossref record for their DOI (title, authors, venue, year; year = Crossref `issued` year). Abstract sources: "Crossref abstract" = abstract in the Crossref record; "OpenAlex abstract" = no abstract in Crossref, so the publisher abstract was read from OpenAlex; "abstract not available" = the claim rests on the title only and should be cited only for the fact stated.

## Recent coseismic landslide events and inventories

- `loi2024noto` (2024): reports landslides triggered by the 1 Jan 2024 Noto Peninsula earthquake (Landslides short report). Abstract not available; cite only as an early documentation of Noto landslides. Section: Introduction (context: recent Pacific Rim events) / Discussion (applicability to new events).
- `zhang2025noto` (2025, issued online Dec 2025; journal volume 23, 2026): spatial analysis of landslides triggered by the 2024 Mw 7.5 Noto earthquake. Abstract not available; title only. Section: Introduction context / Discussion (candidate out-of-sample event for future zero-shot tests).
- `chang2024hualien` (2024): documents coseismic and subsequent landslides of the 3 April 2024 Hualien (M7.2) earthquake, Taiwan. Abstract not available; title only. Section: Introduction context / Discussion (future validation events).
- `chen2025hualien` (2025): preliminary analysis of landslides induced by the 3 April 2024 Mw 7.4 Hualien earthquake. Abstract not available; title only. Section: Introduction context / Discussion-rapid mapping.
- `yilmaz2026kahramanmaras` (2026): releases a coseismic landslide polygon inventory of 20,270 landslides plus a pre-earthquake inventory of 4,495 landslides over about 80,000 km² for the 6 Feb 2023 Kahramanmaraş sequence. Landslides were mapped by expert visual interpretation of 0.3 m post-event aerial photographs and attributed by movement type and material. The authors state that the data support model development, benchmarking and cross-event comparison (Crossref abstract). Section: Introduction (growing inventory base) / Data (candidate additional event) / Discussion (future extension beyond the Pacific Rim).
- `dahal2026insar` (2026): Sentinel-1 InSAR time series over about 66,800 km² of SE Türkiye show that the area affected by slow-moving landslides rose from 12.36 km² before to 159.39 km² after the 2023 Kahramanmaraş earthquakes. Newly activated slopes were linked to steeper terrain across a broad range of shaking, and stabilised slopes to very high shaking on intermediate slopes (Crossref abstract). Section: Discussion (limits of the model: post-seismic and slow-moving responses are outside the coseismic target).
- `grant2025runout` (2025): built a global database of 73,665 measured and estimated coseismic landslide runout lengths from 23 earthquake inventories. Coseismic mobility diverges from aseismic trends and is poorly explained by existing empirical runout models (OpenAlex abstract). Section: Introduction (global inventory compilations) / Discussion (forecast footprints exclude runout; impacts may be under- or over-predicted).
- `gnesko2026kaikoura` (2026): mapped 853 submarine coseismic landslides from the 2016 Kaikōura earthquake across 3.5 km² of the upper Kaikōura Canyon, using deformation-corrected repeat bathymetry. Most were small, shallow debris slides (mean area 2250 m², median depth 4 m, <4% deep-seated) (Crossref abstract). Section: Discussion-NZ implications (onshore model does not cover submarine failures; relevance to tsunami and cable hazard).
- `karantanellis2026kaikoura` (2026): estimated volumes for 1,448 Kaikōura earthquake landslides by differencing satellite-derived (SETSM) DSMs. The volumes were validated against LiDAR, giving volume = 1.99 × area^1.14 for the loss area, and the authors discuss error sources such as vegetation and registration (OpenAlex abstract). Section: Discussion-rapid mapping / Discussion-NZ implications (from area totals to volume).

## Near-real-time and cross-event coseismic models

- `zhang2024nearrealtime` (2024): near-real-time spatial prediction of earthquake-triggered landslides trained on global inventories from 2008–2022. Abstract not available; title only. Section: Introduction gap (global near-real-time models) / Discussion (comparison with global approaches).
- `ma2026crossevent` (2026, online first, no volume yet): seismic landslide controls and cross-event probabilistic assessment modelling in Taiwan using the Chi-Chi and Hualien earthquakes. Abstract not available; title only. Section: Introduction gap (cross-event transfer) / Discussion (comparison with other cross-event studies).
- `endo2026realtime` (2026): for the 2016 Kumamoto earthquake, a 1-Hz PGV map estimated from accelerometers plus GNSS correlated better with landslide distribution than one from accelerometers alone. The paper notes that sparse strong-motion stations leave the spatial ground-motion field uncertain (Crossref abstract). Section: Data (ShakeMap PGA/PGV uncertainty) / Discussion (ground-motion input as an error source).
- `chen2026ebm` (2026): an Explainable Boosting Machine with 9 main effects and 36 pairwise interactions reached test AUC 0.976 for 2022 Luding earthquake landslides. Elevation, PGA and lithology were the top main effects, and lithology × PGA was the strongest interaction (Crossref abstract; single-event, random test split). Section: Methods-HiPhyBoost (interpretable, structured boosting as related work) / Discussion-explainability.

## Physics-guided / Newmark-anchored machine learning

- `zhang2025newmarkxgboost` (2025): hybrid Newmark + XGBoost/RF models for Jiuzhaigou. A Newmark factor-of-safety feature, with Monte Carlo treatment of geotechnical parameters, and Newmark-guided non-landslide sampling improved performance (AUC 0.96). The paper reports AUC 0.88 and 0.86 when applied to Ludian and Luding (Crossref abstract). Section: Introduction gap / Methods-HiPhyBoost (closest prior work on Newmark-informed boosting; HiPhyBoost differs by using monotone constraints and event offsets).
- `liu2025newmarkcnn` (2025, issued online Oct 2025; journal volume 20, 2026): Newmark displacement used as an input feature and for non-landslide sampling in a CNN for Wenchuan. N_CNN outperformed Newmark-integrated LR and RF, and lowering the Dn threshold for sampling improved performance (OpenAlex abstract). Section: Methods-HiPhyBoost (related work on Newmark features in ML).
- `jiang2026frequency` (2026): quantifies frequency requirements for physics-based regional coseismic landslide assessment. Abstract not available; title only. Section: Methods (limits of the simplified Newmark anchor) / Discussion.
- `wang2026regionsimilarity` (2026): a confidence-ellipse region-similarity index used to choose source domains for physics-informed transfer learning (CNN-BiLSTM) in Chongqing. Transfer-learning performance rose with source–target similarity, with large gains when only 1% or 5% of target samples were available (OpenAlex abstract; rainfall/regional, not coseismic). Section: Introduction gap (transfer under domain shift) / Discussion-covariate shift.

## Transfer learning, domain adaptation and validation

- `su2025multisource` (2025): a multi-source domain-adaptation CNN (MDACNN) combined knowledge from two source regions for cross-regional susceptibility mapping in SE coastal China. It reduced bias relative to single-source transfer models, with an average 16.58% improvement across metrics (OpenAlex abstract). Section: Introduction gap / Discussion-covariate shift (pooling multiple source events).
- `wang2026crossregional` (2026): a pre-train/fine-tune transfer-learning CNN-BiLSTM extrapolated across regions better than RF or CNN-BiLSTM trained on enlarged datasets (Wanzhou, Wushan). Performance improved with more source-domain data, and SHAP was used for global interpretation (OpenAlex abstract). Section: Introduction gap / Discussion-covariate shift.
- `kumar2025crossvalidation` (2025): random CV gave performance estimates 6–18% higher than spatial CV for regional landslide susceptibility. Random CV also misrepresented hyperparameter importance and falsely suggested gains from adding variables (Crossref abstract). Section: Methods (justifying leave-one-event-out / spatial evaluation) / Discussion.

## Bayesian / hierarchical models and hazard beyond susceptibility

- `wu2026hierarchical` (2026, online first, no volume yet): hierarchical Bayesian approach to landslide susceptibility in data-sparse regions. Abstract not available; title only. Section: Methods-HiPhyBoost (related hierarchical/partial-pooling work for the empirical-Bayes event prior).
- `dahal2024spacetime` (2024): an Ensemble Neural Network jointly models landslide occurrence and areal density in space and time for the Gorkha-affected region of Nepal, with AUC 0.93 for susceptibility and Pearson r 0.93 for density (Crossref abstract). Section: Introduction (move from susceptibility to hazard including size/density) / Methods (predicting landslide totals).
- `belvederesi2025graph` (2025): a Graph Transformer with a tail-aware loss predicts landslide planimetric area without assuming a Log-Gaussian likelihood, including zeros and very large slides. The distribution of landslide areas from the Wenchuan earthquake was estimated satisfactorily (OpenAlex abstract). Section: Methods (modelling totals and heavy tails) / Discussion-uncertainty.

## Explainable AI

- `wang2024shap` (2024): explainable deep learning for hydro-morphological processes (debris flows to flash floods) across China. Spatial maps of SHAP values gave global and per-unit interpretation (ten-fold CV AUC 0.83–0.86) (OpenAlex abstract; not coseismic). Section: Discussion-explainability (spatial SHAP interpretation; frame as methodological precedent, not as a critique of SHAP stability).

## Rapid post-event mapping

- `xu2026rapid` (2026): compared image differencing, maximum likelihood, object-oriented classification and deep learning for rapid coseismic landslide mapping after the 2018 Mw 6.6 Iburi earthquake. Deep learning gave the best accuracy–speed balance (F1 0.7294, Kappa 0.7139, large-area mapping in 1 min 42 s), and the authors call for better generalisation (OpenAlex abstract). Section: Discussion-rapid mapping (feasibility of the few-tile updating step).
- `fu2026lsdsam` (2026): LSDSAM adapts the Segment Anything foundation model with Landslide71K pre-training, domain-adversarial fine-tuning and few-shot fine-tuning. With eight labelled samples it reached mIoU of 70.7–81.3% on four test sets (OpenAlex abstract). Section: Discussion-rapid mapping (few-shot detection that could supply tiles for Bayesian updating).
- `nasios2026multimodal` (2026): multi-modal landslide detection from Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 optical imagery with multi-encoder vision transformers and ensembling. Abstract not available; title only. Section: Discussion-rapid mapping (cloud-robust SAR+optical mapping).

## Aotearoa New Zealand

- `massey2025gabrielle` (2025): analysis of 116,655 source areas from the >160,000-landslide Cyclone Gabrielle (Feb 2023) inventory. Rain was a main control but with a complex relationship, and 24-h rainfall became less important relative to other factors at very high totals (>220–330 mm) (Crossref abstract; rainfall-triggered). Section: Discussion-NZ implications (national inventory capacity; multi-hazard context).
- `howard2025muriwai` (2025, NZJGG): post-Gabrielle landslide hazard and loss-of-life QRA for Muriwai used RAMMS debris-avalanche simulations calibrated on about 35 recorded large landslides to guide Auckland Council's buy-out scheme (Crossref abstract; rainfall-triggered). Section: Discussion-NZ implications (from regional forecasts to site-specific risk decisions).
- `dreyer2026climate` (2026): under +2 °C warming, a Cyclone Gabrielle-like storm could trigger up to about 90,000 more landslides than in 2023, and the area with extreme landslide densities could grow by up to 34%. Gabrielle itself triggered about 800,000 landslides (Crossref abstract). Section: Discussion-NZ implications (compound seismic–climatic landslide hazard, preconditioning).

## Notes and cautions

- Several 2026 items are online-first with no volume or pages in Crossref (`yilmaz2026kahramanmaras`, `ma2026crossevent`, `wu2026hierarchical`, `xu2026rapid`). Re-check their metadata before submission.
- `zhang2025noto` and `liu2025newmarkcnn` have Crossref issued year 2025 (online) but appear in 2026 journal volumes. The `.bib` uses the issued year, as instructed.
- Several transfer-learning and XAI papers are about rainfall or regional landslides, not coseismic ones (`su2025multisource`, `wang2026crossregional`, `wang2026regionsimilarity`, `wang2024shap`, `massey2025gabrielle`, `howard2025muriwai`, `dreyer2026climate`). Cite them for methodological or regional context only.
- No 2024–2026 paper that specifically critiques SHAP instability in geohazards, and no peer-reviewed landslide inventory for the 2025 Myanmar earthquake, was found in Crossref.
